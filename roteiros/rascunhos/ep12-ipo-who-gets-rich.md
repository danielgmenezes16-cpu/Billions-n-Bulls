[Part 4 – The People Inside]
Like most tech companies, Anthropic pays its employees partly in stock. And going public turns that stock into something they can eventually sell.

<break time="1.0s" />

The filing shows how big that is. About two point nine billion dollars of stock compensation is recognized at the IPO. And around twenty-three point seven billion dollars more over the following two years or so.
Employees usually can't sell right away. IPOs typically come with a lock-up period, often several months, so that insiders don't all rush for the exit on day one.

<break time="1.0s" />

Now for the strange part. For the first half of 2026, Anthropic reported a net loss of about eighty billion dollars.
That sounds catastrophic. But most of it, more than seventy-two billion dollars, came from an accounting rule. Anthropic had sold investors notes that could turn into shares. As the company's value went up, those notes became more valuable, and the company had to record that increase as a loss.
In other words, the better the company did, the bigger the loss on paper.

<break time="1.0s" />

The operating numbers tell a different story. In the second quarter of 2026, Anthropic made an operating profit of about nine hundred seventy-four million dollars. It was the only profitable quarter in the filing, but it was a first.
